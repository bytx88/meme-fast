## Two sides, one changing market
The dev may launch, promote, build, earn fees, sell tokens, reinvest, or leave. Buyers may discover, investigate, buy, hold, add, or sell. These actions can happen in different orders and repeat.

Useful work and credible promotion can bring buyers. Trading can generate fees that fund more work. But the dev may earn from volume while buyers depend on price and enough liquidity to sell. Record both sides to see whether their interests continue to line up.

## The interaction layer
| What you observe | Dev question | Buyer question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work and promotion | What was delivered or paid for? | Is there a current reason to buy? |
| Inventory | What was sold, transferred, or unlocked? | How much could still reach the market? |
| Volume and fees | Who earns, and how much? | Who is trading and what are the costs? |
| Liquidity | Who can withdraw or change it? | What does a size-specific sale quote show? |
| Repeat participation | Does the work bring returning users? | Do buyers keep coming after promotion stops? |

## How to investigate it
Keep a dated table with creator actions, buyer activity, sources, and unresolved questions. Use the same time windows and pool coverage. A release followed by buying is worth investigating, but timing alone does not tell you the release caused the buying.

Check what changes when fees fall, a release is delayed, a competing token attracts buyers, or the group sells more. Write the next observation that would change your view.

## Check your understanding — answered
**The dev is working. Should buyers keep holding?** Recheck the reason for buying, available supply, and trading conditions.

**Buyers are excited. Are interests aligned?** Review control rights, earnings, delivery, and sales.

**Does a productive relationship require constant price rises?** Check work and participation over time; short-term price moves do not settle that question.
