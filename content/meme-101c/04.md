## Supply and powers are separate checks
Existing inventory can be sold; a retained minting power can create more inventory. Transfer controls can affect whether ordinary owners move or sell. A low dev balance does not neutralize those powers.

For a standard Solana token, inspect mint and freeze authorities. For other programs or chains, inspect the exact contract and relevant extensions, roles, upgrade paths, and proxy administration. Names and available controls differ; a scanner summary is a starting point.

| Power | Possible consequence | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mint more units | Dilution and additional sellable inventory | Current mint authority, program rules, enforceable cap, recent issuance |
| Freeze accounts | Affected token accounts cannot perform permitted movements until allowed | Freeze authority and current account status where applicable |
| Blacklist or restrict transfers | Ordinary users may be blocked or face special conditions | Supported contract/program rules, controller, exemptions, current restrictions |
| Change transfer fees | Net receipts or selling costs can change | Current fee settings, caps, change authority, and activation rules |
| Upgrade logic or change roles | Previously checked behavior may change | Upgrade/proxy administrator, governance and delay conditions |

## How many more can they mint?
The current supply is not necessarily the maximum. If a retained authority can mint and no enforceable cap is known, your evidence does not establish a fixed ceiling. If authority is permanently revoked under a standard immutable mechanism, that mint path is disabled. Check other relevant powers and the actual program rather than treating one null field as universal safety.

Decimals set the interpretation of base units; they are not permission to create tokens. A “max supply” number in marketing is weaker than an enforced constraint.

## How to investigate it
Open current mint/contract details and inspect each relevant authority. Check issuance and authority-change transactions. Where applicable, read verified code and admin rights through a supported inspector. Review ordinary-wallet successful sales and failed transaction reasons under the current settings. A route quote or simulation is preliminary evidence, not a guarantee of the next fill.

## Practical case — illustrative
A token has small attributed dev holdings but a retained mint authority with no verified cap. Concentration looks low now, yet future issuance remains possible. Another token has minting revoked but still has a relevant freeze power; its current supply check does not clear transfer risk.

## What does the result mean?
Record each control as verified, active, revoked, or unresolved, with time and source. Recheck after changes. Locking inventory or seeing successful trades does not resolve separate admin powers. No authority concerns does not establish market demand or adequate liquidity.

## Check your understanding — answered
**Supply fixed because the website says so?** Not established. **Minting revoked: no remaining risks?** No. **One exempt address sold: ordinary sellability proven?** No; check applicable rules and actual ordinary transactions.
