## History is evidence, not a permanent dev type
Earlier launches can reveal repeated delivery, transparent allocations, abandoned promises, or supported recurring distribution. Verify that the deployer or team is genuinely connected; a common launch service or funder can create misleading associations.

Do not classify someone forever as builder, distributor, abandoner, or extractor from one transaction. Record observed patterns, outcomes, and confidence in the identity link.

## Reasons each side may change
| Creator-side change | Buyer-side change | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fee income weakens | Participation and attention fade | Current fee receipts, comparable trades, independent discussion |
| Launch or migration fails | Routes or exits are unusable | Platform state, actual transactions, ordinary-wallet execution |
| Another opportunity looks better | A competing token gains participation | Supported new dev activity, exact-contract competition and demand |
| Delivery stops or changes scope | Catalyst ends without renewal | Dated work, missed claims, current primary sources |
| Monetization continues | Buying no longer absorbs sales | Inventory, actual sales, liquidity, participation persistence |

These are hypotheses to investigate, not diagnoses inferred from inactivity alone.

## How to investigate it
Find previous deployments through supported creator records and exact addresses. Check what was promised, delivered, changed, and disclosed. Trace the same relevant controls and inventory under each launch's rules. Do not select only successful or failed examples to fit a label.

For today's token, compare equal windows of attention and market activity with consistent coverage. Separate independent reporting from recycled promotion. Review competing exact contracts and the broader theme; a new token can launch into an exhausted meta.

After migration, ask whether destination trading actually brought continuing participation or merely ended an anticipation event. A successful technical transition is not an automatically renewed buyer cycle.

## Practical case — illustrative
A team still ships releases, but the catalyst's audience rotates to a stronger competitor and buys decline. That can break your demand thesis without proving a rug. Another team repeatedly launches, moves supported inventory, sells into promotion, and leaves promised work unfinished: document the repeated evidence before assigning a history interpretation.
