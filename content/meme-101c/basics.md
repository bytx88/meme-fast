## Start with these terms
| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Token address | The full address identifying the token on its chain. Names and symbols can be copied. |
| Dev or creator | The person or team launching the token. Check which wallets and permissions belong to them. |
| Wallet | An account that can hold assets. One person can use several wallets. |
| Supply | The number of token units. Total issued units, locked units, pool reserves, and units holders can transfer answer different questions. |
| Allocation | Tokens assigned to someone at launch, such as a team or treasury. Record whether they paid for them. |
| Cost basis | What someone paid to acquire their tokens, using a stated accounting method. Missing purchase history means the cost is unknown. |
| Bundle | Transactions submitted together for ordered execution. A scanner may also use this word for launch purchases it detects as coordinated. Check its definition. |
| Linked wallets | Wallets with evidence connecting them. Record confirmed links and suspected links separately. |
| Liquidity pool | Assets held for trading. The pool's reserves and rules affect how much a sale receives. |
| Price impact | How much your trade changes the quoted price. A large sale can receive much less per token than a small one. |
| Permission or authority | A right to change something, such as minting more units or freezing accounts. |
| Vesting and unlock | A schedule releasing tokens over time, and the point when units become available. |
| Creator fee | A share of eligible trading fees paid or accrued to a creator under the platform's rules. |
| Dumping | Selling a large amount quickly enough to put heavy pressure on the market. |

## How to investigate it
Copy the full token address and chain first. Open its explorer page. Identify the creator wallet, ordinary holders, pool accounts, supply, and permissions. Save the links and check time. If a field is unclear, write down the provider's definition before using the number.

## Check your understanding — answered
**Two tokens have the same name. Which one am I checking?** Match the full token address and chain.

**The holder percentage differs between tools. What should I check?** Check their supply definitions, snapshot times, and coverage.

**Is every large holder the dev?** Identify its role and look for ownership evidence. It may be a pool, exchange, or unrelated holder.
