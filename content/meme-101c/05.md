## Total supply is not sellable supply
Providers define total supply, circulating supply, and float differently. Record the definition beside the number. Separate pool reserves, burned units, locked allocations, treasury units, and ordinary balances.

For our example screen, use Chapter 1's available holder units. If restrictions or custody are unclear, record known quantities and leave the float-based percentage unresolved. Transferable units can also be difficult to sell if liquidity is thin.

## What does locked mean?
A token lock or vesting contract controls when specified units become available. A cliff releases an amount on a date. Periodic vesting releases amounts on a schedule. Units may already be claimable while the contract has months left.

A liquidity lock concerns a specified liquidity position. Check both records. Platform rules and project terms determine whether either lock is required.

## How to investigate it
1. Open the public lock/vesting link. Compare its token address and chain.
2. Read contract details in the provider's dashboard. For Streamflow, use the vesting contract details and documented schedule. Research does not require claiming tokens or signing a transaction.
3. Record deposited, withdrawn, unlocked but unclaimed, and still-locked amounts. Check definitions to avoid double counting.
4. Record start, cliff, amount per release, next release, and end with timezone.
5. Check beneficiary, cancellation, and recipient-change rights. Streamflow cancellation, when permitted, can return unvested units to the sender. Include that possibility in your record.

## Practical case — illustrative
After tomorrow's 4m unlock, group available holdings would be 16m and available holder supply 104m, assuming no other changes. **16m ÷ 104m = about 15.4%.** Refresh the calculation after actual transactions.

The release makes tokens available. Watch subsequent transactions to learn whether the team sells them.

## Keep this record
Save an unlock calendar: contract, beneficiary, next release time/timezone, units, claimable amount, controller rights, and source. Separate today's holdings from possible holdings after release.

## Check your understanding — answered
**The lock ends in a year. Can anything be sold today?** Check already claimable units and holdings outside the lock.

**Unlock tomorrow: will the team sell?** Record the amount and watch transactions after release.

**Should treasury units be excluded from float?** Verify restrictions first; the treasury label does not establish a lock.
