# MEME 202A — Snipe Arc: What Makes Price Move?

## Goal
Understand the mechanics that move a meme token during the earliest phase of trading.

Meme 202A sits between basic token knowledge and actual Snipe execution.

Meme 101 answers:

> What am I looking at?

Meme 202A answers:

> Why is it moving?

The Snipe arc focuses on the first minutes and early hours of a token, where liquidity is thin, supply is still changing hands, wallet behavior matters heavily, and attention can accelerate quickly.

This module does **not** focus on mature chart structure, higher-timeframe support/resistance, or swing setups. Those belong in Meme 202B — Swing.

---

# Core Principle

Price moves when the rate of incoming demand exceeds the rate at which available supply is being offered.

In simple form:

**Demand arriving faster than supply → price rises**

**Supply arriving faster than demand → price falls**

The important word is **rate**.

Early meme trading is highly sensitive to:

- how fast buyers arrive
- how large their buys are
- how much supply is available
- who owns that supply
- whether those holders are selling
- whether attention is accelerating

---

# 202A.1 — Price Is an Auction

## What is it?
A meme token trades because buyers and sellers continuously agree on new prices.

The displayed price is usually based on the latest executable trade or pool ratio.

## Why does it matter?
Price does not move simply because "money entered."

It moves because aggressive buying or selling changes the price available for the next trade.

A relatively small amount of capital can move market cap significantly when liquidity is thin.

## What should I understand?
- marginal buyer
- marginal seller
- aggressive buy
- aggressive sell
- last traded price
- pool price
- why market cap can move far more than actual capital inflow

## Beginner mistake
Assuming a $100,000 increase in market cap means $100,000 entered the token.

## Takeaway
**Price moves at the margin.**

---

# 202A.2 — Liquidity & Price Impact

## What is liquidity?
Liquidity is the available capital and token inventory that allows traders to buy and sell.

## What is price impact?
Price impact is how much your trade changes the execution price.

## Why does it matter?
The same $1,000 buy can have very different effects depending on pool depth.

Example:

- Token A has deep liquidity
- Token B has thin liquidity

A $1,000 buy may barely move Token A while pushing Token B sharply higher.

## What should I check?
- pool liquidity
- market cap versus liquidity
- trade size
- expected price impact
- slippage
- available sell liquidity

## Capital Efficiency
A useful Snipe concept is:

**How much price movement does each dollar of net buying create?**

A token that moves 50% on $5,000 of net buying is much more price-sensitive than one requiring $100,000.

## Beginner mistake
Thinking identical buy sizes should create identical price moves.

## Takeaway
**Thin liquidity amplifies marginal capital.**

---

# 202A.3 — Buy/Sell Pressure

## What is it?
Buy/sell pressure is the balance between aggressive demand and aggressive supply.

Transaction count alone is not enough.

Twenty small buys can still be weaker than one large sell.

## Useful model

**Buy pressure = size × frequency × persistence**

**Sell pressure = size × frequency × persistence**

## What should I check?
- total buy value
- total sell value
- average buy size
- average sell size
- transaction frequency
- repeat buyers
- large sells
- whether sellers are being absorbed

## Example
20 buys × $100 = $2,000

1 sell × $5,000 = $5,000

Transaction count looks bullish.

Capital flow is still net bearish.

## Beginner mistake
Counting green transactions without measuring capital.

## Takeaway
**Count capital, not colors.**

---

# 202A.4 — Supply Transfer

## What is it?
Supply transfer is the movement of token inventory from one group of holders to another.

Typical early path:

**Creator / sniper / bundle → early traders → later traders**

## Why does it matter?
Price behavior depends heavily on who currently owns the sellable supply.

Large low-cost holders create potential supply overhang.

Once they sell and their inventory is absorbed, the token can become more responsive to new demand.

## Important concept

**Available supply is not the same as total supply.**

A token may have billions of tokens, but only a small fraction may currently be offered for sale.

If available supply shrinks while demand grows, price can accelerate quickly.

## What should I check?
- large holder balances
- sniper balances
- bundle balances
- creator transfers
- holder turnover
- repeated selling
- whether major sellers are finishing

## Beginner mistake
Looking only at total supply instead of sellable supply.

## Takeaway
**Price rises fastest when new demand meets disappearing supply.**

# 202A.5 — Early Wallet Flow

## Goal
Move from identifying wallet types to understanding what they are doing now.

Meme 101 teaches:

- sniper
- bundle
- creator
- insider
- KOL
- fresh wallet

Meme 202A asks:

> Are they buying, holding, distributing, or leaving?

## Important behaviors
- sniper accumulation
- sniper exit
- bundle push
- bundle distribution
- creator buying
- creator selling
- fresh-wallet swarm
- repeat buyer
- returning buyer
- KOL entry
- KOL exit

## Wallet Quality
Not all inflows are equal.

$5,000 from several proven repeat buyers may carry different information from $5,000 spread across dozens of random fresh wallets.

A useful model is:

**Flow × source quality**

## What should I check?
- wallet history
- funding source
- cost basis
- current balance
- realized profit
- repeat activity
- cluster relationships

## Beginner mistake
Treating every buy as equally informative.

## Takeaway
**Who buys matters alongside how much is bought.**

# 202A.6 — Momentum & Acceleration

## What is momentum?
Momentum is sustained directional activity.

## What is acceleration?
Acceleration means that activity is increasing in speed or strength.

This distinction matters greatly for Snipe.

A token can have high activity without accelerating.

## Bullish acceleration can include
- buyers arriving faster
- average buy size increasing
- sell size decreasing
- holder growth accelerating
- transaction frequency increasing
- larger wallets joining
- social mentions increasing

## Bearish deceleration can include
- smaller buys
- longer gaps between buys
- larger sells
- slowing holder growth
- fewer new wallets
- major holders reducing positions

## Core model

**Demand rising + available supply falling = acceleration**

## Why does it matter?
The chart may still look calm while the underlying engine is strengthening.

Likewise, the chart may still be green while the engine is already weakening.

## Beginner mistake
Waiting for a giant green candle before recognizing momentum.

## Takeaway
**Snipe is often about detecting acceleration before it becomes visually obvious.**

---

# 202A.7 — Attention → Capital

## What is attention?
Attention is exposure: people seeing, discussing, searching, or sharing the token.

## Why does it matter?
Attention alone does not move price.

It matters only when it converts into actual buying.

## Conversion chain

**Exposure**
↓
**Interest**
↓
**Token visit**
↓
**Buy**
↓
**Repeat buying**
↓
**Social proof**
↓
**More exposure**

## Stronger signal
Social mentions rising by themselves are weak.

A stronger combination is:

- social mentions rising
- unique buyers rising
- buy velocity rising
- average buy size rising
- holder count rising

## What should I check?
- independent accounts discussing the token
- engagement quality
- unique buyers
- buyer growth
- average buy size
- repeat buying
- whether social growth continues after price pauses

## Beginner mistake
Treating social activity as equivalent to capital inflow.

## Takeaway
**Attention matters only when it converts into capital.**

# 202A.8 — Push, Hold or Fail

This is the practical state model for early meme price behavior.

---

## PUSH

### Typical conditions
- buy velocity increasing
- available supply shrinking
- new wallets arriving
- major holders not distributing
- transaction frequency increasing

### Result
Price expands.

---

## HOLD

### Typical conditions
- buying slows
- sellers appear
- selling is absorbed
- major holders remain stable
- new buyers continue arriving slowly

### Result
Price establishes a temporary base.

---

## FAIL

### Typical conditions
- buy velocity slows
- sellers increase
- early holders distribute
- new buyers disappear
- liquidity weakens

### Result
Price collapses or loses the previous range.

---

## Healthy continuation

**PUSH → HOLD → PUSH**

This suggests demand paused but remained strong enough to absorb supply.

## Weak continuation

**PUSH → HOLD → DISTRIBUTION → FAIL**

This suggests the first move created exit liquidity for earlier holders.

## Beginner mistake
Assuming every pause after a pump is consolidation.

## Takeaway
**The pause tells you whether supply is being absorbed or released.**

# The Reflexive Meme Loop

Meme tokens can create a feedback loop.

## Positive loop

**Attention**
↓
**Buyers arrive**
↓
**Capital enters**
↓
**Available supply is consumed**
↓
**Price rises**
↓
**Price attracts more attention**
↓
**More buyers arrive**

This can create rapid acceleration.

---

## Negative loop

**Price stalls**
↓
**Attention slows**
↓
**New capital slows**
↓
**Early holders sell**
↓
**Available supply increases**
↓
**Price falls**
↓
**More holders exit**

This can unwind just as quickly.

---

# Core Snipe Questions

Before entering an early meme token, ask:

1. Is demand accelerating?
2. Is available supply shrinking?
3. Who is creating the buying pressure?
4. Are major low-cost holders still holding?
5. Are bundles pushing or distributing?
6. Are fresh wallets truly independent?
7. Are large sells being absorbed?
8. Is attention converting into actual buying?
9. Is the token in PUSH, HOLD, or FAIL?
10. If current buyers stop, what supports the price next?
