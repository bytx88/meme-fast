# MEME 202B — Swing Arc: Where Does Price Move Next?

## Goal
Understand how a meme token behaves after the initial launch phase, when price history, holder rotation, supply zones, and base formation begin to matter.

Meme 202B sits after Meme 202A — Snipe.

Meme 202A answers:

> Why is price moving now?

Meme 202B answers:

> After the first move, where is price likely to move next?

The Swing arc focuses on tokens that have survived the initial launch and have enough history to develop:

- recognizable price structure
- trapped holders
- profit-taking zones
- holder rotation
- accumulation and distribution
- new valuation ranges
- continuation or failure

This module does **not** focus on the first minutes of launch mechanics. Those belong in Meme 202A — Snipe.

---

# Core Principle

A swing opportunity appears when old supply has been absorbed, ownership has rotated, and new demand can move price again.

In simple form:

**Old supply clears + new holders absorb + demand returns → continuation**

**Old supply remains + demand weakens + distribution continues → failure**

The Swing trader is trying to understand whether the market has built enough structure for another expansion.

---

# 202B.1 — Price Structure

## What is it?
Price structure is the pattern created by repeated buying and selling over time.

Once a token survives the launch phase, individual transactions matter less than the structure created by many transactions.

## Common structures
- higher highs
- higher lows
- lower highs
- lower lows
- ranges
- compression
- expansion
- failed expansion
- previous highs
- previous lows

## Why does it matter?
Price leaves memory.

If many traders bought around a certain level, that area can matter later because those holders remember their entry.

Likewise, traders trapped higher may sell when price returns near breakeven.

Structure reflects where traders became committed.

## What should I check?
- previous highs and lows
- repeated reaction zones
- whether lows are rising or falling
- whether highs are expanding or compressing
- whether price is accepting a new range

## Beginner mistake
Treating support and resistance as arbitrary chart lines.

## Takeaway
**The chart is a map of where traders became committed.**

---

# 202B.2 — Base & Repricing

## What is a base?
A base is a price region where selling repeatedly meets enough buying to prevent further decline.

## What is repricing?
Repricing happens when the market accepts a materially different valuation range.

Example:

**Old base: $250k–$300k**

Price expands.

Early holders sell.

New buyers absorb.

**New base: $450k–$550k**

The market is now trading around a higher accepted valuation.

## Pump vs Repricing

### Pump
Price moved somewhere.

### Repricing
Price moved somewhere and stayed there.

## What should I check?
- repeated testing of the new range
- time spent in the range
- whether large sellers are being absorbed
- holder turnover
- liquidity stability
- whether old support has moved higher

## Beginner mistake
Calling every large move a repricing.

## Takeaway
**Repricing requires acceptance.**

---

# 202B.3 — Supply Zones

## What is a supply zone?
A supply zone is a price area where holders are likely to sell meaningful inventory.

## Where can supply come from?
- previous buyers trying to break even
- early holders taking profit
- whales distributing
- bundle inventory
- KOL entries
- previous highs
- trapped buyers

## Why does it matter?
Resistance exists because someone has a reason to sell.

Example:

A token previously reached $1M and fell to $400k.

When price returns toward $900k–$1M, traders trapped near the old high may sell to recover capital.

That creates supply.

## What should I check?
- previous high-volume areas
- old highs
- known whale or bundle entry levels
- large-holder cost basis
- repeated selling near the same zone

## Beginner mistake
Treating resistance as purely technical geometry.

## Takeaway
**Resistance exists because somebody has inventory they want to sell.**

---

# 202B.4 — Demand & Absorption

## What is demand?
Demand is buying interest capable of taking available supply.

## What is absorption?
Absorption happens when buyers repeatedly take sell pressure without allowing price to collapse.

## Why does it matter?
Strong swing structure is often visible in how price reacts to selling.

Example:

- first $10k sell causes -20%
- later $10k sell causes -7%
- later $10k sell causes -2%

The market may be getting stronger even though large sells continue.

## What should I check?
- size of repeated sells
- price reaction after each sell
- speed of recovery
- whether the same buyer is absorbing everything
- whether seller size is shrinking
- whether the base holds

## Sell Impact
A useful concept is:

**Sell size relative to price damage**

If equal-sized sells create less downside over time, absorption may be strengthening.

## Beginner mistake
Looking only at green candles instead of how price handles red flow.

## Takeaway
**Strength is shown by how price reacts to selling.**

---

# 202B.5 — Holder Rotation

## What is holder rotation?
Holder rotation is the transfer of ownership from one group of holders to another.

Example:

**Early holders**
- dev
- sniper
- bundles
- first traders

sell into:

**New holders**
- later buyers
- swing traders
- stronger holders
- new KOLs
- broader retail

## Why does it matter?
A token may fall sharply while ownership becomes healthier underneath.

Example:

**$100k → $2M → $500k**

The chart looks damaged.

But if most early low-cost inventory has exited and new holders accumulated around $400k–$600k, the average cost basis may have risen significantly.

That changes future sell pressure.

## What should I check?
- top-holder changes
- realized profit
- old-wallet exits
- new-holder entries
- new-holder cost basis
- bundle remnants
- concentration changes

## Important warning
Ownership can appear more distributed while one actor is simply moving tokens across related wallets.

## Beginner mistake
Assuming a lower top-holder percentage automatically means healthier ownership.

## Takeaway
**A chart can look the same while the ownership underneath it has completely changed.**

---

# 202B.6 — Expansion & Pullback

## What is expansion?
Expansion is a rapid move where aggressive demand consumes available supply.

## What is a pullback?
A pullback is a decline after expansion as profit-taking and new selling appear.

A common healthy sequence is:

**Base → expansion → pullback → absorption → continuation**

## Healthy pullback
Typical signs:
- volume cools
- sell size decreases
- important holders remain
- previous breakout area holds
- new buyers enter lower
- price stabilizes

## Dangerous pullback
Typical signs:
- whale distribution
- holder count falls
- liquidity leaves
- previous base fails
- selling accelerates
- rebounds become weaker

## Why does it matter?
The percentage decline alone does not tell you whether the move is healthy.

## Beginner mistake
Treating every 30–50% drop as either automatically bullish or automatically dead.

## Takeaway
**Judge a pullback by who is selling and who is absorbing it.**

# 202B.7 — Continuation vs Distribution

This is the key swing decision.

Two tokens can both look like:

**Pump → consolidation**

But the underlying structure can be completely different.

---

## Continuation Structure

Typical conditions:
- early holders already reduced
- new buyers are accumulating
- selling pressure is decreasing
- holder base is expanding
- liquidity is stable
- price responds well to new demand

### Result
The market may be preparing another expansion.

---

## Distribution Structure

Typical conditions:
- whales slowly reduce positions
- retail buys every dip
- top wallets keep selling
- volume remains high
- price struggles to advance
- concentration appears lower because inventory is being unloaded

### Result
The range may be functioning as exit liquidity.

---

## Effort vs Result

A very useful Swing concept:

### High effort, weak result
Large buying volume produces little upside.

Possible meaning:

**Heavy supply is being distributed into buyers.**

### Low effort, strong result
Relatively small buying volume moves price strongly.

Possible meaning:

**Available supply is thin.**

## Beginner mistake
Assuming consolidation is automatically accumulation.

## Takeaway
**Ask how much price movement the buying effort produces.**

# 202B.8 — Swing Lifecycle

A meme swing can be understood as a lifecycle.

---

## Stage 1 — Discovery

Early participants find the token.

---

## Stage 2 — Expansion

Attention and capital accelerate.

---

## Stage 3 — First Distribution

Early holders realize gains.

---

## Stage 4 — Repricing

The market searches for a sustainable new valuation.

---

## Stage 5 — Base Formation

New holders absorb available supply.

---

## Stage 6A — Continuation

Demand returns.

**Base → breakout → expansion**

---

## Stage 6B — Failure

Demand does not return.

**Base → distribution → breakdown**

---

## Why does it matter?
The same chart pattern can mean different things depending on where the token sits in the lifecycle.

The Swing trader is trying to determine:

> Has enough supply transfer happened to support another expansion?

## Beginner mistake
Using the same strategy at every lifecycle stage.

## Takeaway
**Know where you are in the move before deciding what comes next.**

## Healthy path

**Expansion**
↓
**Profit taking**
↓
**Supply transfer**
↓
**Absorption**
↓
**New base**
↓
**Reaccumulation**

**Next expansion**

## Failure path

**Expansion**
↓
**Profit taking**
↓
**Distribution**
↓
**Weak demand**
↓
**Base failure**
↓
**Capitulation**

# Core Swing Questions

Before entering a meme swing, ask:

1. What structure has price created?
2. Has the market accepted a new valuation?
3. Where is meaningful supply waiting?
4. Are large sells being absorbed?
5. Have early holders already distributed?
6. Has ownership rotated into stronger hands?
7. Is the pullback healthy or structural failure?
8. Is buying effort producing meaningful price progress?
9. Is the current range accumulation or distribution?
10. Has enough supply cleared for another expansion?

For the Swing trader, the goal is:

**Determine whether the post-expansion structure is building the foundation for another move or becoming exit liquidity for existing holders.**
