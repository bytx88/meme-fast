# MEME 101 — Level 8: Trade and Survive

## Goal
Turn all previous knowledge into practical risk management.

---

## 1. Position Size

### What is it?
Position size is how much capital you allocate to a trade.

### Why does it matter?
Meme-token risk is often nonlinear. Thin liquidity can turn a normal-sized trade into a difficult exit.

### What should I check?
- Maximum acceptable loss
- Token liquidity
- Market cap
- Volatility
- Exit capacity

### Beginner mistake
Sizing only from conviction.

### Takeaway
**Position size should reflect risk and liquidity, not excitement.**

---

## 2. Stop Loss

### What is it?
A stop loss is a predefined condition for exiting a losing position.

### Why does it matter?
It prevents one trade from becoming an uncontrolled loss.

### Meme-specific issue
Thin markets can move through a stop level quickly or trigger it on brief volatility.

### What should I check?
- Liquidity
- Volatility
- Position size
- Whether the stop is price-based or thesis-based

### Takeaway
**A stop is useful only if the market can execute your exit.**

---

## 3. Scaling In

### What is it?
Scaling in means entering a position in smaller pieces rather than all at once.

### Why does it matter?
It reduces the risk of committing all capital at a poor entry.

### What should I check?
- Planned entry zones
- Maximum total position
- What evidence justifies each additional buy

### Beginner mistake
Calling repeated averaging down "scaling in" without a plan.

### Takeaway
**Every additional entry should have a reason.**

---

## 4. Scaling Out

### What is it?
Scaling out means selling a position in stages.

### Why does it matter?
It allows you to realize gains while retaining exposure if the move continues.

### What should I check?
- Profit targets
- Liquidity
- Remaining thesis
- Major resistance areas

### Takeaway
**You do not need to predict the exact top to exit well.**

---

## 5. Taking Initial Capital Out

### What is it?
This means selling enough after a favorable move to recover some or all of your original capital.

### Why does it matter?
It reduces the amount of principal still exposed.

### Beginner mistake
Assuming the remaining position is risk-free; it can still lose value.

### Takeaway
**Recovering principal changes your risk, not the token's risk.**

---

## 6. Risk / Reward

### What is it?
Risk/reward compares what you may realistically lose with what you may realistically gain.

### Why does it matter?
A token with huge theoretical upside may still be a poor trade if downside is immediate and exit liquidity is weak.

### What should I check?
- Entry
- Invalidation
- Realistic target
- Liquidity
- Probability of each outcome

### Beginner mistake
Using 100x as the target simply because another meme once achieved it.

### Takeaway
**Potential upside is only one side of the trade.**

---

## 7. Survivorship Bias

### What is it?
Survivorship bias occurs when traders focus on the tokens or wallets that succeeded and ignore the many that failed.

### Why does it matter?
Looking only at famous 50x or 100x winners makes meme trading appear easier than it is.

### What should I check?
- Full trading history
- Failed launches
- Losing wallets
- Tokens that never recovered

### Takeaway
**Winners are visible; failures disappear.**

---

## 8. Exit Strategy

### What is it?
An exit strategy defines when and why you will reduce or close the trade.

### Possible triggers
- Target reached
- Thesis broken
- Major holder distribution
- Liquidity deterioration
- Narrative failure
- Better opportunity elsewhere

### Beginner mistake
Planning the entry in detail but leaving the exit to emotion.

### Takeaway
**Know what makes you leave before you enter.**

---

## 9. Entry Liquidity vs Exit Liquidity

### What is it?
Entry liquidity determines how easily you can build a position. Exit liquidity determines how easily you can leave later.

### Why does it matter?
A token may be easy to buy during hype and very difficult to sell after attention disappears.

### Takeaway
**Always ask who will buy from you later.**

---

## 10. The Core Meme-Trading Question

Before every trade, ask:

**Who owns the supply, what did they pay for it, what are they doing now, and who will buy it from them?**

That question connects:

- valuation
- liquidity
- ownership
- bundles
- wallet behavior
- price action
- narrative
- risk management

---

# Final 8-Level Framework

## Level 1 — Read the Token
Market cap, liquidity, volume, holders, supply, price impact.

## Level 2 — Understand the Launch
Bonding curves, graduation, DEXs, snipers, fresh wallets, slippage.

## Level 3 — Understand Ownership
Top holders, dev wallets, insiders, bundles, wallet clusters, KOL wallets.

## Level 4 — Read Wallets
Cost basis, realized profit, accumulation, distribution, absorption, organic demand.

## Level 5 — Read Price Behavior
Base, resistance, breakout, repricing, pullback, panic dump, exit liquidity.

## Level 6 — Understand Attention
Narrative, catalysts, social momentum, KOL shilling, rotation, chain narratives.

## Level 7 — Detect Danger
Liquidity control, rugs, honeypots, mint/freeze authority, wash trading, hidden distribution.

## Level 8 — Trade and Survive
Position sizing, stops, scaling, taking profit, risk/reward, survivorship bias, exits.

---

# Beginner Rule

**Do not ask only, "Can this token go up?"**

Ask:

**Who owns it?
Who is buying it?
Who is selling it?
How much liquidity exists?
And who will take the other side when I want to exit?**
