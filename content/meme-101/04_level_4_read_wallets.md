# MEME 101 — Level 4: Read Wallets

## Goal
Move beyond holder percentages and understand what important wallets are actually doing.

---

## 1. Cost Basis

### What is it?
Cost basis is approximately how much a wallet paid for its tokens.

### Why does it matter?
A wallet holding $20,000 may have spent $18,000 or only $500. Their incentive to sell is very different.

### What should I check?
- Initial purchase amount
- Average entry
- Current position value
- Amount already sold
- Remaining unrealized profit

### Beginner mistake
Judging risk only from current wallet value.

### Takeaway
**Cheap inventory creates powerful potential sell pressure.**

---

## 2. Realized vs Unrealized P&L

### What is it?
Realized profit has already been taken. Unrealized profit still sits in the remaining position.

### Why does it matter?
A wallet that has recovered its original capital can behave very differently from one still risking principal.

### What should I check?
- Total amount invested
- Total amount sold
- Remaining position
- Profit already locked in

### Beginner mistake
Assuming a wallet still holding tokens is still fully committed.

### Takeaway
**A holder may already be playing entirely with profit.**

---

## 3. Accumulation

### What is it?
Accumulation happens when wallets steadily acquire supply.

### Why does it matter?
Persistent accumulation can reduce available sell supply and strengthen a base.

### What should I check?
- Repeated buys
- Wallet balance growth
- Dip absorption
- Number of independent accumulating wallets
- Whether sellers are shrinking

### Beginner mistake
Calling every sideways chart accumulation.

### Takeaway
**Accumulation means supply is actually being absorbed.**

---

## 4. Distribution

### What is it?
Distribution is the gradual selling of inventory into incoming demand.

### Why does it matter?
Price can remain strong while major holders quietly reduce exposure.

### What should I check?
- Large-wallet balances declining
- Transfers into fresh wallets
- Selling into green candles
- Strong volume with weak price progress
- Concentration changes

### Beginner mistake
Waiting for a giant red candle before recognizing distribution.

### Takeaway
**Distribution can happen while the chart still looks strong.**

---

## 5. Supply Absorption

### What is it?
Absorption occurs when buyers repeatedly take available sell supply without allowing price to collapse.

### Why does it matter?
It helps show whether sellers are being cleared out.

### What should I check?
- Repeated sells near the same price
- Price recovery after sells
- Shrinking seller size
- Strong bids or repeated buys
- Whether the same buyer is doing all the absorbing

### Beginner mistake
Confusing one large support buy with broad absorption.

### Takeaway
**Healthy absorption survives repeated selling.**

---

## 6. Organic Buying

### What is it?
Organic buying comes from independent market participants rather than one coordinated cluster.

### Why does it matter?
A move is generally more durable when demand broadens.

### What should I check?
- Number of unique buyers
- Funding independence
- Holder growth
- Distribution across wallets
- Volume after major wallets stop buying

### Beginner mistake
Treating all buy transactions as separate demand.

### Takeaway
**Durability improves when buying spreads across independent participants.**

---

## 7. Paper Hands

### What is it?
Paper hands are short-term holders that quickly sell when price moves against them or when small profit appears.

### Why does it matter?
They create repeated supply during early price discovery.

### What should I check?
- Short holding times
- Repeated small sells
- Sell clusters after minor dips
- Whether weak holders are gradually being cleared

### Beginner mistake
Assuming every early seller is bearish.

### Takeaway
**Weak supply can be cleared before a stronger base forms.**

---

## 8. Conviction Holders

### What is it?
Conviction holders tolerate larger fluctuations and hold through normal volatility.

### Why does it matter?
Stable holders can reduce immediate sell pressure.

### What should I check?
- Holding duration
- Whether balances remain stable
- Whether they add on weakness
- Whether they previously sold large portions

### Beginner mistake
Assuming long holding automatically means strong conviction.

### Takeaway
**Conviction is behavior over time, not a wallet label.**

---

## Level 4 Checklist

For important wallets, ask:

1. What did they pay?
2. How much profit have they already realized?
3. Are they adding or reducing?
4. Is supply being absorbed?
5. Is new buying independent?
6. Are weak holders being replaced by stronger ones?
