# MEME 101 — Level 1: Read the Token

## Goal
Learn the basic numbers shown on a meme-token page before looking at narratives, charts, or wallets.

---

## 1. Market Cap

### What is it?
Market cap is the token price multiplied by circulating supply.

### Why does it matter?
Token price alone tells you very little. A token priced at $0.0001 can still have a large valuation if billions of tokens exist.

### What should I check?
- Current market cap
- Previous market-cap highs
- Circulating supply
- Whether supply can increase
- Market cap relative to liquidity

### Beginner mistake
Assuming a low token price means more upside.

### Takeaway
**Judge valuation by market cap, not token price.**

---

## 2. Liquidity

### What is it?
Liquidity is the capital available for traders to buy and sell the token.

### Why does it matter?
A token can have a high market cap but very little liquidity. Thin liquidity makes price easier to move and exits harder.

### What should I check?
- Total liquidity
- Market cap versus liquidity
- Whether liquidity is locked or burned
- How much a large sell would move price
- Whether liquidity is growing

### Healthy structure
Growing market cap together with growing liquidity.

### Dangerous structure
Large market cap with extremely thin liquidity.

### Beginner mistake
Assuming a $5M market cap means $5M can be withdrawn.

### Takeaway
**Market cap shows valuation. Liquidity shows how easily you can exit.**

---

## 3. Liquidity vs Market Cap

### What is it?
This compares the token's valuation with the amount of real trading liquidity supporting it.

### Why does it matter?
Two tokens can both have a $1M market cap, but one may have $200k liquidity while another has only $20k.

### What should I check?
- Liquidity as a percentage of market cap
- Whether liquidity grows as market cap grows
- Sell impact for large holders
- Whether liquidity is concentrated in one pool

### Beginner mistake
Comparing market caps without comparing liquidity.

### Takeaway
**The same market cap can carry very different exit risk.**

---

## 4. Volume

### What is it?
Volume measures how much trading occurred during a period.

### Why does it matter?
High volume shows activity, but activity is not the same as genuine demand.

### What should I check?
- Volume relative to liquidity
- Volume relative to market cap
- Whether volume persists
- Buy versus sell activity
- Number of unique wallets trading

### Warning
Bots and wash trading can inflate volume.

### Beginner mistake
Treating high volume as proof of strong conviction.

### Takeaway
**Volume measures trading, not conviction.**

---

## 5. Holders

### What is it?
Holders are wallets currently owning the token.

### Why does it matter?
Holder growth can show spreading ownership, but one person can control many wallets.

### What should I check?
- Total holders
- Holder growth
- New holders versus exits
- Holder concentration
- Whether wallets are related

### Healthy structure
Holder count rises while concentration falls.

### Beginner mistake
Assuming 5,000 wallets means 5,000 independent investors.

### Takeaway
**Count ownership, not just wallets.**

---

## 6. Token Supply

### What is it?
Supply tells you how many tokens exist, how many circulate, and whether more can be created.

### Why does it matter?
Supply structure affects valuation, dilution risk, and holder concentration.

### What should I check?
- Total supply
- Circulating supply
- Burned supply
- Mintable supply
- Team or reserved allocations

### Beginner mistake
Looking only at current circulating supply.

### Takeaway
**Know how much supply exists and whether that number can change.**

---

## 7. Price Impact

### What is it?
Price impact is how much an order moves the token price.

### Why does it matter?
A trade that looks small in dollar value can move a thin meme market significantly.

### What should I check?
- Expected price impact before buying
- Expected price impact before selling
- Pool depth
- Position size relative to liquidity

### Beginner mistake
Sizing a trade from account balance instead of available liquidity.

### Takeaway
**Your order can become part of the market move.**

---

## Level 1 Checklist

Before going deeper, answer:

1. What is the market cap?
2. How much liquidity supports it?
3. Is volume persistent or temporary?
4. Is ownership spreading?
5. Can supply increase?
6. How much would a meaningful buy or sell move price?

If you cannot answer these, you are still reading the surface of the token.
