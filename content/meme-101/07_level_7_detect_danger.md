# MEME 101 — Level 7: Detect Danger

## Goal
Recognize structural and behavioral risks that can destroy a trade regardless of the narrative.

---

## 1. Liquidity Lock / Burn

### What is it?
Liquidity-provider tokens may be locked, burned, or left under someone's control.

### Why does it matter?
If a creator can remove liquidity, traders may suddenly lose the ability to exit normally.

### What should I check?
- LP ownership
- Lock duration
- Burn status
- Whether new pools can be created or drained

### Beginner mistake
Assuming a "locked liquidity" label means all liquidity risk is gone.

### Takeaway
**Understand who controls the pool and for how long.**

---

## 2. Rug Pull

### What is it?
A rug pull is a deliberate extraction of value by creators or insiders.

### Common forms
- Removing liquidity
- Dumping concentrated supply
- Hidden minting
- Manipulated taxes
- Misleading token mechanics

### Beginner mistake
Thinking rugs always happen instantly.

### Takeaway
**Some rugs are sudden; others are slow distributions.**

---

## 3. Honeypot

### What is it?
A honeypot is a token structure that allows buying but prevents or severely restricts selling.

### Why does it matter?
The chart can look strong because trapped buyers cannot exit normally.

### What should I check?
- Successful independent sells
- Contract restrictions
- Taxes
- Blacklist behavior
- Trading simulation where available

### Beginner mistake
Assuming a rising chart proves the token is tradable.

### Takeaway
**Always verify that ordinary wallets can sell.**

---

## 4. Mint Authority

### What is it?
Mint authority determines whether additional tokens can be created.

### Why does it matter?
New supply can dilute existing holders or be dumped into liquidity.

### What should I check?
- Whether mint authority exists
- Who controls it
- Whether it has been revoked
- Supply history

### Takeaway
**Unlimited new supply can destroy scarcity.**

---

## 5. Freeze Authority

### What is it?
Freeze authority may allow an operator to restrict token accounts.

### Why does it matter?
It can create control over who can transfer or trade.

### What should I check?
- Whether freeze authority exists
- Whether it has been revoked
- Whether wallets have previously been restricted

### Takeaway
**Trading freedom depends partly on token permissions.**

---

## 6. Wash Trading

### What is it?
Wash trading is artificial activity created by repeatedly trading among related wallets.

### Why does it matter?
It can make a token look more active and liquid than it really is.

### What should I check?
- Repetitive trade sizes
- Same wallets buying and selling
- High volume with weak holder growth
- Circular funding

### Beginner mistake
Treating all volume as organic participation.

### Takeaway
**Volume can be manufactured.**

---

## 7. Market Maker / Bot Activity

### What is it?
Bots may continuously buy and sell to create liquidity, maintain spreads, or influence market appearance.

### Why does it matter?
Automated activity can make a token appear more active than its human demand really is.

### What should I check?
- Repetitive timing
- Repetitive size
- Symmetrical buy/sell behavior
- Volume after bots disappear

### Beginner mistake
Assuming every bot is malicious.

### Takeaway
**Understand whether activity represents real new demand or automated churn.**

---

## 8. Hidden Distribution

### What is it?
Hidden distribution happens when large holders sell through many wallets or gradually transfer inventory before selling.

### Why does it matter?
Top-holder concentration can appear to improve while the same actor is still controlling the supply.

### What should I check?
- Transfers to fresh wallets
- Common funding
- Repeated sell patterns
- Clusters of small sales
- Balance reductions across related wallets

### Beginner mistake
Assuming concentration fell because ownership became healthier.

### Takeaway
**Distribution can be disguised as decentralization.**

---

## 9. Unlocked-Liquidity Warning

### What is it?
An unlocked-liquidity warning means someone may still have control over the LP position.

### Why does it matter?
They may be able to remove liquidity, making exits much harder.

### What should I check?
- Who controls LP tokens
- Lock status
- Lock expiry
- Pool ownership changes

### Takeaway
**Liquidity risk is ownership risk.**

---

## Level 7 Checklist

Before entering, ask:

1. Can ordinary wallets sell?
2. Who controls liquidity?
3. Can more supply be minted?
4. Can wallets be frozen?
5. Is volume organic?
6. Are bots creating most activity?
7. Are insiders distributing through fresh wallets?
