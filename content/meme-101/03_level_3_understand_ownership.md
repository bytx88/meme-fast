# MEME 101 — Level 3: Understand Ownership

## Goal
Learn who really controls the token supply and whether apparent decentralization is genuine.

---

## 1. Top Holders

### What is it?
Top holders are the wallets owning the largest portions of token supply.

### Why does it matter?
A small number of wallets may control enough supply to overwhelm liquidity.

### What should I check?
- Top 10 holder percentage
- Top 20 holder percentage
- LP, burn, exchange, and system wallets
- Changes in concentration over time

### Beginner mistake
Counting LP or burn wallets as ordinary holders.

### Takeaway
**Know who actually controls sellable supply.**

---

## 2. Dev / Creator Wallet

### What is it?
The creator wallet is associated with the person or group that launched the token.

### Why does it matter?
The creator may own cheap inventory or control related wallets.

### What should I check?
- Initial creator allocation
- Current holdings
- Transfers to other wallets
- Previous selling
- Related wallets

### Beginner mistake
Checking only the visible creator wallet.

### Takeaway
**Follow where the creator's tokens went.**

---

## 3. Insiders

### What is it?
Insiders are wallets or people that may have early information, privileged access, or direct links to the launch.

### Why does it matter?
They may enter before the wider market understands the token.

### What should I check?
- Entry timing
- Funding links
- Similar trade behavior
- Connections to creator wallets
- Selling around announcements

### Beginner mistake
Calling every successful early buyer an insider.

### Takeaway
**Early success is a clue; wallet relationships provide stronger evidence.**

---

## 4. Bundles

### What is it?
A bundle is a group of wallets that may actually belong to or coordinate with the same actor.

### Why does it matter?
Many wallets can create the appearance of broad ownership while control remains concentrated.

### What should I check?
- Bundle percentage
- Common funding
- Entry timing
- Similar transaction size
- Transfers between wallets

### Beginner mistake
Treating every holder as independent.

### Takeaway
**One operator can look like many holders.**

---

## 5. Bundle Push

### What is it?
A bundle push happens when coordinated wallets buy aggressively and rapidly push price upward.

### Why does it matter?
The move can look like broad organic demand even when a small group created most of the pressure.

### What should I check?
- Which wallets created the move
- Whether those wallets are linked
- Supply accumulated during the push
- Whether independent buyers followed
- Whether the bundle began selling afterward

### Healthier path
Bundle accumulation → push → organic takeover → broader ownership.

### Dangerous path
Bundle accumulation → push → retail chase → bundle distribution.

### Takeaway
**After the push, ask who is buying next.**

---

## 6. Wallet Clusters

### What is it?
A wallet cluster is a group of addresses that may belong to one actor based on funding, transfers, timing, or behavior.

### Why does it matter?
Clustering can reveal hidden concentration.

### What should I check?
- Shared funding source
- Repeated transfers
- Similar timing
- Similar order size
- Shared destination wallets

### Beginner mistake
Treating one matching behavior as proof of common ownership.

### Takeaway
**Clustering is strongest when several links point to the same conclusion.**

---

## 7. Wallet Funding

### What is it?
Wallet funding shows where a wallet received the capital used to trade.

### Why does it matter?
Multiple wallets funded from the same source may be related.

### What should I check?
- Funding wallet
- Timing
- Amount
- Whether funds were split across several wallets
- Whether the same source repeatedly funds new accounts

### Beginner mistake
Stopping at the first funding transaction.

### Takeaway
**Follow the money backward before judging wallet independence.**

---

## 8. Smart Money / KOL Wallets

### What is it?
These are wallets tracked because they belong to known traders, influential accounts, or historically profitable participants.

### Why does it matter?
Their activity can reveal attention shifts, but following them blindly is dangerous.

### What should I check?
- Actual entry price
- Current position size
- Previous win/loss history
- Whether they are adding or reducing
- Whether you are already late

### Beginner mistake
Copying the wallet after most of the move has already happened.

### Takeaway
**A good trader's position is information, not an instruction.**

---

## Level 3 Checklist

Ask:

1. Who owns the supply?
2. Which wallets are truly independent?
3. Are creator or insider-linked wallets still holding?
4. How much supply belongs to clusters?
5. Did bundled wallets create the current move?
6. Are influential wallets entering early or already distributing?
