# MEME 101 — Level 5: Read Price Behavior

## Goal
Understand what the chart is showing about supply, demand, and changing valuation.

---

## 1. Support / Base

### What is it?
A support area or base is a price region where selling repeatedly meets enough buying to prevent further decline.

### Why does it matter?
A stable base can become the foundation for the next move.

### What should I check?
- Number of successful retests
- Volume around the base
- Holder behavior
- Whether large sellers are being absorbed

### Beginner mistake
Drawing support from a single bounce.

### Takeaway
**A base becomes meaningful through repeated defense.**

---

## 2. Resistance / Ceiling

### What is it?
Resistance is an area where selling repeatedly overwhelms buying.

### Why does it matter?
Large holders may use these areas to exit.

### What should I check?
- Repeated rejection levels
- Selling volume
- Wallet distribution near the level
- Whether each rejection becomes weaker

### Beginner mistake
Assuming price must break resistance because it reached it several times.

### Takeaway
**A ceiling remains a ceiling until demand proves otherwise.**

---

## 3. Breakout

### What is it?
A breakout occurs when price moves beyond an established resistance area and sustains the move.

### Why does it matter?
A successful breakout can indicate a change in supply-demand balance.

### What should I check?
- Volume
- Follow-through
- Retest behavior
- New buyers
- Whether major holders are selling into it

### Beginner mistake
Buying the first candle above resistance.

### Takeaway
**A breakout needs acceptance, not just a wick above resistance.**

---

## 4. Fake Breakout

### What is it?
A fake breakout is a move above resistance that quickly fails.

### Why does it matter?
It often traps late buyers and provides liquidity for sellers.

### What should I check?
- Lack of follow-through
- Immediate heavy selling
- Weak holder growth
- Large wallets reducing positions
- Price falling back below the old ceiling

### Beginner mistake
Assuming every breakout candle starts a new trend.

### Takeaway
**Failure to hold the breakout matters more than briefly crossing the level.**

---

## 5. Repricing

### What is it?
Repricing happens when the market establishes a materially different valuation range.

### Why does it matter?
After a major move, the token may stop trading around its old base and begin forming a new one.

### What should I check?
- New support range
- Holder turnover
- Liquidity growth
- Whether old sellers have cleared
- Whether demand persists without artificial support

### Beginner mistake
Assuming every pump is a repricing.

### Takeaway
**Repricing requires the market to accept the new valuation.**

---

## 6. Pullback

### What is it?
A pullback is a temporary decline after an upward move.

### Why does it matter?
Healthy markets rarely move upward in a straight line.

### What should I check?
- Depth of the decline
- Volume during the decline
- Whether major holders are dumping
- Whether support holds

### Beginner mistake
Treating every red move as the end of the trend.

### Takeaway
**A pullback tests whether buyers still exist below the highs.**

---

## 7. Panic Dump

### What is it?
A panic dump occurs when selling overwhelms available liquidity very quickly.

### Why does it matter?
Thin meme markets can collapse far faster than traders expect.

### What should I check?
- Who started selling
- Whether large wallets joined
- Liquidity depth
- Whether buyers continue absorbing
- Whether the token recovers after the first wave

### Beginner mistake
Assuming a low market cap automatically means price cannot fall much further.

### Takeaway
**When liquidity disappears, price can gap downward rapidly.**

---

## 8. Entry Liquidity

### What is it?
Entry liquidity is the available sell supply that allows you to build a position without pushing price excessively.

### Why does it matter?
Entering too aggressively can create your own bad average price.

### What should I check?
- Pool depth
- Available sellers
- Price impact
- Position size

### Takeaway
**A good thesis can still have a bad entry.**

---

## 9. Exit Liquidity

### What is it?
Exit liquidity is the future buying demand available when you want to sell.

### Why does it matter?
Paper profit is only useful if enough buyers exist to take the other side.

### What should I check?
- Current liquidity
- Trading activity
- Position size
- Large-holder exits
- Whether attention is growing or fading

### Beginner mistake
Planning upside without planning the exit.

### Takeaway
**Your exit requires someone else to buy.**

---

## Level 5 Checklist

Ask:

1. Where is the real base?
2. Where does supply repeatedly appear?
3. Is a breakout being accepted?
4. Is the token repricing or only pumping?
5. Are pullbacks being absorbed?
6. Could your position exit without overwhelming liquidity?
